\section{The Incidents}

This PIR covers two related email incidents. INC0352662 concerns
outgoing email from internal UTS Linux applications not being received
by vendors (``outgoing''), INC0351580 covers emails in and out of a
mailing list management system (``listserv'').

Both incidents occured after the decommissioning of Mimecast from the
UTS email ecosysten. This work occurred in two stages, DNS changes to
point email away from Mimecast systems and towards Microsoft systems
on 22nd August (CHG0037414) and the switchoff of Mimecast services on
Thursday 10th September (by Mimecast, since we'd paid for service up
until but not beyond that date).

\section{Root Causes}

\subsection{Assumptions}

%FIXME: itemize three or four assumptions??

Simplistic assumptions made the work seem a lot simpler than it turned
out to be. It was assumed that the changes at the Linux side would be
entirely at the DNS level; change the MX (Mail eXchanger) records for
several domains like chere.uts.edu.au and socs.uts.edu.au,
watch those changes propagate to the Internet and go home on a
Saturday afternoon.

It was also assumed that Abnormal was a drop-in replacement for
Mimecast. Whatever Mimecast had done for us in the past, Abnormal
would do after the cutover.

\subsection{Changes wide apart}

The DNS changes just mentioned were done on August 22nd while Mimecast
service was switched off on September 10. It's normally a great
idea to get part of the work done well ahead of time, but in this
instance a twenty-day gap between the DNS changes and the Mimecast
switchoff meant a lot of effort was needed to get back into an
email-fix frame of mind and the August changes were not in our recent
memory.

\subsection{No Mimecast involvement}

I'm guessing that any support contract UTS had with Mimecast had
expired well before service was switched off and it would have been
expensive and awkward to engage their support just as we
were finishing our association with them. The fact remains that
we were unsure just what Mimecast did and had to re-discover this
behaviour in an incident situation.

\subsection{Non-Standard Configurations}

Understanding the UTS email system was made more difficult due to some
non-standard configuration. For a while the listserv.uts.edu.au server
was considered to be external to UTS because mail generated in the UTS
network for listserv was routed out to Mimecast and not kept internal
to the UTS network. This anomaly has been fixed and internal mail to
listserv is now routed directly to that server.

The listserv postfix configuration itself hadn't been touched in
thirteen years! Postfix configuration for endpoints is trivially
simple so it was ``set and forget''. After the Mimecast switchoff the
listserv configuration was changed to present a server certificate to
external-source emails.

Another non-standard configuration, handled before August 22nd and
fully functional both before and after the Mimecast switchoff, was the
handling of the fhrmail@uts.edu.au email as an alias for a
production @neomail.uts.edu.au.

\section{What went wrong}

I was quite surprised that the initial focus of the incidents was
on the listserv issue and not outgoing email to UTS and the Internet
in general. Why were tickets on failed email lists growing in number
while nobody was reporting the (to my mind) larger issue of missing
outgoing emails?

It turns out the problem of missing outgoing emails was reported, but
ServiceNow automatically closed the ticket. A Service Now analyst
correctly asked the reporter for more information, the reporter
gave the information needed (but using a direct Service Now interface)
and the ticket stayed in ``Customer On-Hold'' status for
three days and was auto-closed. The issue was not surfaced to the
Linux team until days after the listserv issue.

Certificates (Felix Wu) and Identity (Iain Rayner).
The MIM bridge on the listserv issue was led by Steve Dean and the

The postfix email program is a best-of-breed Mail Transfer Agent for
Linux (and before then, UNIX) systems. While UTS runs an old versions on
an old versions of RedHat Enterprise Linux, Postfix handled
client-side and server-side certificate management with a simple
configuration change. Postfix is easy to configure and during the
incident we were able to apply changes and have them in effect in the
space of minutes.

The GNU mailman program is the earliest open source program still in
widespread use to be written in Python. 

\section{Primary Recommendations}

I offer three recommendations that will help prevent issues the next
time there is a major email change.

\subsection{Document email flows on a single Confluence page}

All end-to-end email flows need to be documented on a single
Confluence page.

\begin{itemize}
  \item Internal Linux to UTS emails
  \item Internal Linux to Internet emails
  \item Internal Linux to listserv emails
  \item Internet email to listserv emails
% FIXME: is Internal Linux to neomail a thing??
  \item Internal Linux to neomail
  \item Internet email to neomail
  \item Internet to UTS emails
  \item UTS to Internet emails
\end{itemize}

``Internal Linux'' collectively encompasses several systems; building
management, Boomi, Cognos, Dell and Neo.

\subsection{Document Abnormal}

A separate Confluence page should explain the role of Abnormal in the
UTS email ecosystem.

\subsection{Establish standard email addresses}

The standard email addresses hostmaster@uts.edu.au (for Internet users
to report DNS issues) aud postmaster@uts.edu.au (for Internet users to
report email issues) should be set up. These email addresses can be
done as Microsoft DLs (Distribution Lists), exclusively to @uts.edu.au
adddresses, that grow and shrink over time as staff join and leave.

\section{Secondary recommendations}

These recommendations should occur through normal quarterly planning
processes.

\subsection{Replace listserv email lists with Microsoft DLs}

While GNU mailman does its job well, more people in an enterprise will
be familiar with Microsoft Distribution Lists. Indeed, some listserv
mailing lists were already migrated to DLs by Suggi and Jorge. Mailman
provides a web frontend to messages which DLs probably don't.

\subsection{Upgrade Linux postoffice servers and postfix versions}

The two postoffice Linux servers should be upgraded to RHEL 9 and the
standard postfix version provided with that version of RedHat
Enterprise Linux.

\subsection{Review the role of cluebringer}

A large Perl application, cluebringer, is used in the UTS mail
ecosystem for rate limiting emails and other functions. This
functionality should be reviewed and understood.

\subsection{Audit failing emails twice a year}

UTS conducts an audit of their Linux systems twice a year. I recommend
that a small additional task be added to the audit: look at the
mail logs on Postoffice and find failing emails and remediate
emails to departed employees and non-existant addresses.
